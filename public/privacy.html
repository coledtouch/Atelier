Effective October 11, 2026

Privacy Policy

Atelier (“Atelier”, “we”, “us”) is a personal AI assistant at atelier.ciprari.ai. This policy explains what data Atelier accesses and how it is used. It says where that data is stored and who it is shared with. It also explains how you can delete it.

At a glance: Atelier accesses your data only to do what you ask. The only additions are four things that keep it working. They are cookieless page-view counts, web-font downloads, security reports and app error reports (see Technical data). Atelier never sells your data or uses it for advertising. It never uses your data to train AI models. Data from Google APIs is handled under Google’s Limited Use requirements (see Google user data).

1. Who can use Atelier

Atelier is a private application. The owner signs in with a passcode. A limited number of invited testers (currently 25 spots) can sign in with LinkedIn. There is no other sign-up. Atelier is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from them.

2. Data we access and collect

Information you provide

  • Prompts and conversations — the questions, instructions, text and images you enter.
  • Video Remix — the owner’s Labs feature. Testers don’t have it. You attach a video in Video mode and send an instruction. The clip and your instruction then go to Google’s Gemini to plan the edit. The clip goes through Gemini’s Files API, as when you ask about a video. Nothing is filmed or paid for until you approve the plan. If the approved plan includes new shots, Atelier sends each shot’s text prompt. For some shots it also sends one still frame taken from your video. This goes to Gemini Omni (which keeps it for up to 55 days: see section 6). When you pick a Runway model, it goes to Runway instead. That includes Google’s Veo 3.1, xAI’s Grok Imagine and ByteDance’s Seedance 2.5 through Runway. The edit itself happens entirely in your browser. That covers decoding, drawing captions and cards, and encoding the MP4. It uses Mediabunny, an open-source library served from Atelier’s own site. Mediabunny makes no network requests of its own. The video is never uploaded for cutting.
  • Voice dictation — on an iPhone or iPad, your mic recording is sent to OpenAI. The same happens wherever your browser can’t transcribe speech itself. The mic may open from your tap. Talk to Atelier can also open it for you. So can “Start listening when I open Atelier”. Google’s Gemini gets the recording instead when OpenAI is busy, unavailable or can’t read it. The recording is sent only to turn it into text in your message box. Atelier’s servers store neither the recording nor a separate copy of the transcription. If you send the text, it becomes part of your conversation. It then follows the thread-storage choices below. On Android and desktop, your browser’s own speech recognition is used first, under its maker’s terms.
  • Look up — a short phrase you select in a finished answer is sent to Atelier’s server. The phrase can be up to 120 characters long. The server asks Wikipedia for a public summary and preview image (see Sharing). Depending on your Look up setting, this happens automatically, only on tap, or never.
  • Profile and memory — optional information you add in the “You” panel. That means your name, a description of yourself, writing samples, a style guide and remembered facts. It also holds facts Atelier notes from your conversations. Atelier never notes facts from text that arrived through a link or a share. You can view and delete them.
  • Imported history — you can import a ChatGPT/Claude data export or Claude Code session files. Atelier reads the messages you wrote in them to build your profile. The files are opened in your browser and are never uploaded or stored as files. To build the profile, Atelier sends the messages you wrote to an AI model for analysis. They go newest first, each cut to its first 900 characters. The total is up to about 150,000 characters for testers and 280,000 for the owner. The model is Google’s Gemini when it is available, otherwise another model listed under Sharing. Claude Code imports also send the model the last folder name of up to 25 projects. These are projects you worked in. The model also gets how many prompts each had, for example “Acme-Site (212 prompts)”. The drive, the folders above it and your home folder are never sent. Your home folder’s name is usually your computer’s user name. The model returns a profile, writing style and remembered facts. These are saved like the rest of your “You” panel. That means on the server, so they sync between your devices. For testers, they go in your tester record. Nothing else from the files is sent or kept by this profile import.
  • Imported Claude chats — you can choose Settings → Your data → Import Claude chats. Atelier opens your claude.ai data export in your browser. It saves each conversation as a thread on this device. A thread holds your messages and Claude’s replies and reasoning. It also holds the names of attached files and a short excerpt of their extracted text. Each of these is cut to a size limit. Nothing is sent to an AI model while importing. These imported threads sync to your other devices when thread sync is enabled. For testers, private sync is off until you explicitly enable it. Afterwards they are treated like your other threads. When you continue one, its earlier turns go to the model you are using. Questions to the accounts agent about your Claude history work the same way. The passages it finds go to the agent’s model. Atelier never signs in to claude.ai or reads it directly.
  • Voluntary feedback — if you choose Send feedback, Atelier stores your message and category. That lets the studio owner read and respond to bugs, confusing behavior and suggestions. You may choose to attach a PNG, JPEG or WebP screenshot under 350 KB. You may also include basic diagnostics: the active mode, app version, online status and screen size. Prompts, conversation contents and private connected-account details are not automatically collected with feedback. Review your message and screenshot before submitting them. They contain whatever you choose to include.

If you sign in as a LinkedIn tester

  • Your profile, style and memory — what you keep in the “You” panel. It holds your name, a description of yourself, writing samples and a style guide. It also holds learned facts and remembered items. It is stored on the server in your tester record. That lets Atelier use it in your answers.
  • Your threads — conversations and generated media are saved in a browser database for your LinkedIn account. They are kept separate from other accounts on that browser. Private thread sync is off until you explicitly enable it. With your consent, threads and their images and videos are also saved in cloud storage. Threads in the previous shared browser database stay local unless you explicitly select and import them. Importing adds them to your account.

  • Voice dictation — recordings are transcribed as described above and are not stored. The usage ledger records only what each one cost.
  • Look up — free: it never touches your usage ledger. It starts on “On tap”. A phrase you select is then sent only when you tap Look up. You can change this in Settings. Its answer and preview image are cached briefly at Cloudflare for you alone. See “Look-up cache” in section 6.
  • Video files — a video you attach for analysis may be uploaded to Google’s Gemini Files API. Google holds it for up to 48 hours and then deletes it.
  • Sign-in cookies — two strictly necessary cookies. __Host-atelier_tester keeps you signed in for up to 30 days. __Host-atelier_li lasts at most 10 minutes. It is used only to finish the LinkedIn sign-in round trip. Neither is used for tracking.

Technical data

  • Our host, Cloudflare, processes standard request data, such as IP address and request logs. It does so to operate and secure the service. We use IP addresses to block repeated wrong-passcode attempts. We also use them to limit repeated sign-in attempts, security reports and app error reports. For a wrong passcode, Atelier keeps a one-way keyed code for the network it came from. The network is an IPv4 address, or the first half of an IPv6 address. Atelier keeps only that code and a count of failures. It never stores the address itself. This record is deleted 15 minutes after that network’s last failure, once any lockout has ended. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies. Besides the tester sign-in cookies above, there is one more strictly necessary cookie. It is for the studio owner only. __Host-atelier_owner is set when the owner’s passcode is accepted and lasts up to 30 days. It keeps the owner’s own devices working while a passcode lockout holds off someone else’s guesses. It holds only an expiry date and a signature. It is never used for tracking. Clearing the passcode in Settings, or Clear this device, removes it. If it is ever sent with a wrong passcode, Atelier keeps a one-way code of it. Atelier never keeps the cookie itself. The code is kept until the date the cookie would have expired. That stops that copy of the cookie from being used again.
  • Page analytics — Atelier’s pages load Cloudflare Web Analytics, which counts visits. It is a small script from static.cloudflareinsights.com. It sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device (no local storage). Per Cloudflare, it does not fingerprint you by your IP address, browser details or anything else. For each page view it reports the page address and the site that referred you. It also reports your country, browser, operating system and device type (desktop, mobile or tablet). It reports how fast the page loaded, too. The owner sees only aggregate statistics in Cloudflare’s dashboard. Those are visits, page views, top pages, referring sites, countries and browsers. They are never linked to your sign-in, your prompts or your tester record. The owner can switch it off in Cloudflare at any time.
  • Fonts — Atelier’s pages, this one included, load their typefaces from Google Fonts. The stylesheet comes from fonts.googleapis.com and the font files from fonts.gstatic.com. Your browser fetches them directly from Google, before you sign in and on each page load. So Google receives your IP address and your browser details (user agent). It also receives the address of the site that asked for them. Google Fonts sets no cookies. Atelier sends Google nothing else for this. A Build preview you run may likewise load fonts and code libraries. They can come from Google Fonts or the jsDelivr and unpkg CDNs.
  • Security reports — Atelier’s pages carry a Content Security Policy. A page may try to load something the policy doesn’t list. Your browser may then send Atelier a short automatic report. The browser’s report can include the full page address and the page you came from. It can also include a snippet of the blocked code. Atelier discards all of that. It records one line per report, and that line holds only these details. First come the policy rule involved and the blocked item’s website (domain only). In place of a website it may hold a word such as “inline”. Then come the Atelier page and script file, without anything after “?” or “#”. Last come a line and column number, and whether the policy was enforced. Atelier does not record your IP address with it. It also leaves out browser or device details (user agent), the referring page and query strings. Nor does it record any page content. The report is never linked to your sign-in. Your IP address is used only briefly, as a counter key. That key stops one connection from flooding reports. These lines are kept in Cloudflare’s Worker logs for at most 7 days. They are used only to keep Atelier secure and working.
  • App error reports — some failures make the page send Atelier a short automatic report. That happens if Atelier fails to start or takes more than 20 seconds to open. It also happens if Atelier hits an error it didn’t catch. The report lets the owner fix it. It holds the app version, the kind of failure and the error’s type name. A type name is something like “TypeError”. It says which Atelier file and line the error happened in. It can add up to six more places in Atelier’s own files that led there. It names the kind of device and app. Examples are an iPhone Home Screen app, an Android app or a desktop browser. It says how far Atelier had got in starting and whether the device was online. It says whether Atelier’s offline copy (its service worker) was in use. When a file didn’t load, it says whether that looked like a dropped connection. It never includes the error’s message, your prompts or conversations, or anything you typed. It never includes a page address with anything after “?” or “#”. Atelier does not record your IP address or browser details (user agent) with it. The report is never linked to your sign-in. A report from the studio owner’s own device sends the owner’s passcode with it. That report is only marked as the owner’s. That way made-up reports can’t push it off the list. Your IP address is used only briefly, as a counter key. That key stops one connection from flooding reports. It is also used so one network can’t crowd out everyone else’s reports. For that, Atelier keeps a one-way keyed code of the network (never the address itself). The code is kept only until that hour is over (at most about 70 minutes). A device sends at most five a session. Each report leaves one line in Cloudflare’s Worker logs, kept for at most 7 days. The studio owner can see recent reports in Settings.

How Google user data is used

  • Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing features you request. For email, that is searching, reading and summarizing. Saving drafts and sending need your approval. For Calendar, it is listing events and, with your approval, creating or deleting them. For Drive, it is searching and reading files and, with your approval, creating a Google Doc. For Photos, it is attaching the photos you pick.
  • Email, calendar, Drive and photo content needed for a request goes to an AI provider. That is the AI model provider selected for that request (see Sharing). It is sent solely to generate the response you asked for. That provider may hold it briefly in its prompt cache. See “Prompt caches at AI providers” in section 6. An answer may be read aloud with an AI voice. That happens because you tapped Read aloud, or automatically because you asked out loud. Then the answer’s text is also sent to Google’s Gemini speech API to make the audio. That text includes any email, calendar or Drive details the answer mentions.
  • It is never used for advertising and never sold. It is never used to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine-learning models. Humans do not read it unless you explicitly ask for help with a specific message. Humans may also read it when necessary for security or legal compliance. They may also read data that is aggregated and anonymized for internal operations.

How Google user data is stored

  • Atelier stores the OAuth refresh token (and a short-lived access token) in encrypted Cloudflare storage. That lets it call Google on your behalf. Our servers store email, calendar and Drive content only where the owner’s own answers quote it. Those quotes sit inside the owner’s synced threads. A photo you pick from Google Photos is attached to your message. It is resized to at most 1280 pixels. It also syncs with the owner’s thread to the owner’s private Cloudflare R2 storage. Deleting the thread doesn’t remove that copy automatically (see “The studio owner’s threads” in section 6).
  • Answers that quote or summarize this content are saved in your conversation history in your browser. So are photos you attach. They are also saved, through the owner’s thread sync, in the owner’s private Cloudflare storage. Delete threads in the app (section 8). Deleting a thread leaves copies of attached photos and of long texts (over about 32 KB). They stay in the owner’s private storage until the owner removes them there. See “The studio owner’s threads” in section 6.
Limited Use disclosure. Atelier’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

4. How we use data

  • To answer your requests and perform the actions you ask for. That covers chat, code, images, video, ideas, apps and account tasks.
  • To personalize answers using your profile, memory and writing style.
  • To keep the service secure and working, for example by rate-limiting passcode attempts and diagnosing errors.
  • To see, in aggregate, which pages are used and how fast they load. This uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, without cookies.
  • For testers: to enforce each tester’s spending allowance and the shared monthly tester budget.
  • To sync threads across devices when enabled, and to review feedback you voluntarily submit.

We do not use your data for advertising, profiling for third parties, or selling.

6. Where data is stored and for how long

  • In your browser: conversations, generated media and settings, kept until you delete them. Use Settings → Your data → Clear this device, or delete individual threads. Tester threads use a local database tied to the signed-in LinkedIn account. Threads left in the previous shared database remain local unless you explicitly select and import them.
  • The studio owner’s threads: the owner’s own threads, including their images and videos, also sync privately. They go to the owner’s Cloudflare storage, behind the owner’s passcode, until the owner deletes them. The original of a video attached to a question isn’t synced. Only the frames and preview Atelier took from it are synced. On a phone whose browser reports mobile data or Data Saver, a Wi-Fi rule applies. Videos over 10 MB wait for Wi-Fi before they upload or download. The owner can turn on “Download videos on mobile data” to skip the wait. Chrome on Android reports this. Safari on iPhone doesn’t, so there videos sync on any network. A deleted thread can be restored for 30 days. After that its saved copies are removed, except copies of images, videos and long texts. Long texts are over about 32 KB, such as long answers or pasted documents. Those copies are not removed automatically. One copy can be shared by several threads. So they stay in that private storage until the owner deletes them there. Tester sync uses a separate namespace and does not give testers access to the owner’s threads.
  • Tester thread sync: off until you explicitly enable private sync. Synced conversations, imported chats and media are held in private Cloudflare R2 storage. That storage is kept for your LinkedIn account. It has a 1 GiB limit that counts everything kept there, and up to 500 threads. That includes the saved copies described below. The same media and mobile-data rules described for owner sync apply. Turning sync off in Settings → Your data stops new transfers but keeps existing cloud copies. Clear this device removes local data only. Signing in to the same LinkedIn account brings synced copies back when sync is enabled. A synced thread deleted from the Threads drawer can be restored for 30 days. Atelier keeps a copy of a deleted thread for restoring. It also keeps earlier versions of edited or restored threads. Both are removed 30 days after they were saved. A restored thread’s copy goes when you restore it. Deleting a thread leaves its copies of images, videos and long texts (over about 32 KB). They stay in that private storage and count toward the limit. They stay until the entire tester cloud namespace is removed. That happens when the tester record expires after 90 days of inactivity, or sooner on request. Cloud clean-up can fail or need more than one pass. In that case the server retries it before deleting the tester record.
  • Voluntary feedback: the message, category, any screenshot you chose and any basic diagnostics you included. They are kept in Cloudflare storage for up to 90 days after submission. They have an expiry set when each submission is saved. Older submissions may be removed sooner to keep the feedback inbox within its limits. Only the studio owner can read the feedback inbox.
  • App error reports: at most 50 distinct reports are kept in Atelier’s Cloudflare storage. Each stays until 30 days after that failure was last seen. The same failure again only adds to a count. When the list is full, a report not from the owner’s own devices makes room first. Among those, the one seen fewest times and longest ago goes before the others. Only the studio owner can read them.
  • Video Remix (this device): remix files are kept in this browser’s IndexedDB (keys starting rx:). They are the attached video (up to 500 MB), new shots and screenshots you add. Plan drafts and finished cuts are kept there too. The video copy, drafts and cuts are removed once the remix hasn’t changed for 7 days. That is checked when Atelier opens. New shots, which were paid for, stay while their thread exists. Everything is removed once its thread is deleted. Shots still being filmed are collected first, so their cost settles. Settings → Your data → Clear this device removes them all. None of these files is synced on its own. Only the plan, settings, new shots’ status and small preview stills travel with the owner’s thread. A finished cut under 16 MB is also added to its thread. It then appears in the Library and syncs like any other video in a thread. A bigger cut stays on this device.
  • Read aloud clips: audio made for Read aloud is cached only on this device. It uses browser Cache Storage, up to about 30 MB or 200 clips. It stays until Settings → Your data → Clear this device clears it. It is also cleared when a tester signs out.
  • Build app data (this device): what a Build app you made saves while you use it. This is its “local storage”. It is kept in this browser’s IndexedDB for that app. It is kept separately for each account on the browser. The limit is 5 MB per app and 50 MB in all. Every version of the app shares it. It isn’t synced or sent to Atelier’s server. It isn’t put in thread backups or added to a downloaded app. The app’s card shows how much it holds, and Clear app data removes it. Deleting a thread removes its apps’ data. That happens at once when the thread can’t be restored. When it can be restored, the data goes 30 days after the thread left this device. That covers Recently deleted, or a thread deleted on another device. So a restore brings the data back with it. Clear this device removes all of it. The app runs in a sandbox and never sees Atelier’s own data or another app’s.
  • Dictation recordings: never saved. A recording stays in the page’s memory only until it has been transcribed. If sending it fails, it stays until you record again or close Atelier. Atelier’s server passes it to the transcription provider without storing it.
  • Quick launch (this device only): this covers what you share to Atelier from another app. It also covers what a website sends to it the same way. Either waits in browser Cache Storage until Atelier opens it. A share holds at most one video or 4 photos, plus its text. It can be used for 30 minutes. That limit is checked when Atelier reads it. Nothing deletes it on a timer. It is deleted once opened, the next time Atelier opens after that, or on sign-out. Clear this device deletes it too. A message you typed or dictated but haven’t sent is kept in this browser’s local storage. That way it survives the phone reopening Atelier. It can come back for 6 hours. It is deleted once sent, the next time Atelier opens, or when you sign out. Clear this device deletes it too. A shortcut, a link’s text or a share can arrive while you are signed out. That launch can continue for 15 minutes after you sign in. It is deleted when you next sign in or open Atelier signed in. Clear this device deletes it too. Your Quick launch choices stay in this browser until you sign out or clear this device. So does the private key of a “Send without a tap” link. The key is also in the link you paste into your Shortcut. Each Shortcut run opens that link with your words in it. Your browser’s History (and History sync) keeps the key and those words. Examples of History sync are iCloud and Chrome sync. If anyone else can see that history, use Settings → Quick launch → New link. Atelier Assist, the Android side-button app, works the same way. Each request opens Atelier at a link with that key and your words in it. So Chrome’s History (and Chrome sync) keeps them too. New link under Quick launch → Atelier Assist retires that key. Atelier Assist turns your voice into text with your phone’s speech service. That service may use Google’s or your phone maker’s servers before the words reach Atelier. The app itself keeps only the key. Nothing here reaches Atelier’s server unless sent as a message, and the key never does. There is one exception. A share may arrive while Atelier’s offline helper (its service worker) isn’t running. For example, this happens just after you clear this site’s data. It also happens when a very large video stops the helper. Such a share is sent by your browser to Atelier’s server instead. The server discards it without reading or storing it and asks you to share again.
  • Prompt caches at AI providers: Atelier makes follow-ups in a thread cheaper and faster. To do that, it keeps the start of each request unchanged. That start is the request’s instructions and the earlier turns of that thread. For Anthropic’s Claude models, Atelier marks it so the provider can reuse it. Anthropic keeps that cached copy for up to 1 hour after it was last used. For some requests that is 5 minutes. OpenAI, Google, xAI, Z.ai and Meta cache repeated request starts automatically for minutes to hours. DeepSeek caches them for up to a few days. This follows each provider’s own policies. A cached start can include what the accounts agent read for that answer. An email it opened is one example. A cached copy can only be reused by requests from Atelier’s own account with that provider. The provider deletes it when it expires.
  • Look-up cache (Cloudflare): the Wikipedia answer to a look-up is kept for up to 24 hours. That answer repeats the phrase you selected, up to 120 characters. It is kept for 1 hour when Wikipedia has no article for it. Its preview image is kept for up to 7 days. Both sit in the Cloudflare edge cache of the data centre that served you. This applies to testers and the owner alike. It is stored separately from your profile and memory, optional synced threads and voluntary feedback. Each entry is filed under a one-way (SHA-256) hash. The hash covers who asked, the phrase and the Wikipedia language. For an image, it covers who asked and the image’s file name. So an entry is only ever served back to the same person. No other Atelier user can see or detect what you looked up. Nothing else from the answer you selected it in is kept.
  • Cloudflare (server): your profile/memory (so it syncs between your devices) and connection tokens. They are kept until you delete or disconnect them.
  • Tester records: your LinkedIn ID, name, email and photo link, and your usage ledger. The record also holds your profile, style and memory. All of it expires after 90 days without using Atelier, or sooner on request. Any tester cloud-sync namespace is purged before its expired tester record is deleted. Clean-up is retried if it fails or needs more than one pass. On request, the owner’s Delete data (in the owner’s Testers panel) removes a tester at once. It removes their sign-in sessions and the whole cloud-sync namespace. That namespace holds threads, media, deleted threads and earlier versions. It also removes the tester record, usage ledger, profile, style and memory. It removes any refused sign-in note naming them, and their voluntary feedback. A tab of theirs still open may send a little more in the following minute. So the same deletion runs once more 10 to 20 minutes later. Until then only their LinkedIn ID is kept, for that second pass. With no record left, that person can sign in again later as a new tester. They can do so while a spot is free, even if their access had been revoked.
  • Refused tester sign-ins: a LinkedIn sign-in can be turned away. That happens if all spots are taken, tester access is paused, or your access was revoked. Atelier then keeps a note of the most recent such attempt. It holds the LinkedIn member ID, name and profile photo link LinkedIn returned, and the time. The owner can see it in the Testers panel and decide whether to open a spot. Your email address is not part of it. Only the latest attempt is kept, and a newer one replaces it. It is deleted 7 days after the attempt, or sooner on request.
  • Gemini Files API: videos uploaded for analysis are deleted by Google within 48 hours.
  • Gemini Omni (video): Google keeps each Omni video request for up to 55 days. That covers its text prompt, the finished clip, and any starting image or still frame. Still frames are taken from your video. Google keeps them so a clip can be edited or extended later. Atelier doesn’t ask Google to delete it sooner. This applies to the owner’s and testers’ Omni videos. It also applies to Video Remix shots made with Omni.
  • Runway (owner only): Runway’s links to finished videos expire within 48 hours. Atelier keeps its own copy on the owner’s device. It sits in the owner’s thread, which syncs to the owner’s private Cloudflare storage. Atelier’s server keeps no other copy.
  • xAI (owner only): xAI’s links to finished Grok videos are temporary. Atelier keeps its own copy of a Grok image or video on the owner’s device. It sits in the owner’s thread, which syncs to the owner’s private Cloudflare storage. Atelier’s server keeps no other copy.
  • AI providers: besides the storage listed above, providers may retain request data for a limited time. That storage is prompt caches, the Gemini Files API, Gemini Omni, Runway and xAI. They do so under their own API policies, for example for abuse monitoring. They do not use it for advertising.
  • Operational logs: short-lived request logs kept by Cloudflare for reliability and security. Atelier’s own log lines, including security reports, are kept for at most 7 days.
  • Page analytics: Cloudflare Web Analytics data is kept by Cloudflare under its own retention. The owner sees it only as aggregate page-view and page-speed statistics in Cloudflare’s dashboard. Nothing is stored on your device for it.

7. Security

Traffic is encrypted with HTTPS. Browsers are told to use only HTTPS for Atelier (HSTS). API keys and account tokens are stored as encrypted server-side secrets. They are never sent to your browser. Access requires the owner’s passcode or a LinkedIn tester sign-in. Repeated wrong passcodes from one network trigger an automatic lockout. There is also a cap on wrong attempts from all networks together. Tester sessions are stored only as a one-way hash. Testers can never reach the owner’s accounts, tools or profile. Actions that send, post, pay or change something require your explicit approval.

8. Your choices and deletion

  • Delete conversations and media: delete threads in the app, or Settings → Your data → Clear this device. With thread sync on, Clear this device leaves synced copies in private cloud storage. Delete synced threads from the Threads drawer instead. They can be restored for 30 days from Settings → Your data → Recently deleted.
  • Turn tester sync off: Settings → Your data → Sync across your devices. This stops new transfers without deleting your cloud copies. To resume, sign in to the same LinkedIn account and enable sync. Threads that never synced stay on the device where they were made. They leave it only if you export or import them.
  • Delete profile and memory: edit or remove entries in the “You” panel.
  • Disconnect Google: Settings → Connections → Disconnect (this deletes the stored token). You can revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions as well or instead.
  • Disconnect Canva: Settings → Connections → Disconnect. This revokes Atelier’s access at Canva and deletes the stored tokens.
  • Disconnect other services: revoke the token in that service’s settings. Atelier then loses access immediately.
  • Testers: sign out in Settings → Access. After 90 days of inactivity, Atelier removes your tester record, usage ledger and profile. It also removes any private cloud-sync namespace. Cloud clean-up is retried before the expired record is deleted. To request earlier account and cloud-data deletion, email cole@ciprari.ai. The owner’s Delete data then removes your record, sessions, usage ledger, profile, cloud-sync namespace and feedback. It removes them from the server at once, and once more 10 to 20 minutes later. See “Tester records” in section 6. On a device you used as a tester, the sign-in screen offers a button. It reads “Remove my tester data from this device”. It shows whenever tester data is still there, also while you can’t sign in. For example, tester access may be closed. It deletes the threads, Build app data, settings and Read aloud clips kept there for testers. So does clearing this site’s data in your browser. You can also remove Atelier from your LinkedIn account’s permitted services.
  • Request deletion of everything: email cole@ciprari.ai. We will delete server-side data within 30 days.
