Privacy Policy

Atelier (“Atelier”, “we”, “us”) is a personal AI assistant available at atelier.ciprari.ai. This policy explains what data Atelier accesses, how it is used, where it is stored, who it is shared with, and how you can delete it.

In short: Atelier accesses your data only to do what you ask it to do, plus the cookieless page-view counts, web-font downloads and security reports that keep it working (see Technical data). It never sells your data, never uses it for advertising, and never uses it to train AI models. Data from Google APIs is handled under Google’s Limited Use requirements (see Google user data).

1. Who can use Atelier

Atelier is a private application. The owner signs in with a passcode. Tester access is currently closed: LinkedIn sign-in is switched off, existing tester sessions no longer work, and no one can sign up. When tester access is open, a limited number of invited testers (25 spots) can sign in with their LinkedIn account; there is no other sign-up. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

2. Data we access and collect

Information you provide

  • Prompts and conversations — the questions, instructions, text and images you enter.
  • Video Remix (the owner’s Labs feature; testers don’t have it) — when you attach a video in Video mode and send an instruction, the clip and your instruction are sent to Google’s Gemini to plan the edit (the clip goes through Gemini’s Files API, as when you ask about a video). Nothing is filmed or paid for until you approve the plan. If the approved plan includes new shots, each shot’s text prompt — and, for some shots, one still frame taken from your video — is sent to Google’s Gemini Omni, or to Runway when you pick a Runway model (including Google’s Veo 3.1 and xAI’s Grok Imagine through Runway). The edit itself (decoding, drawing captions and cards, encoding the MP4) happens entirely in your browser with Mediabunny, an open-source library served from Atelier’s own site that makes no network requests of its own: the video is never uploaded for cutting.
  • Voice dictation — on an iPhone or iPad, or wherever your browser can’t transcribe speech itself, a recording you make with the mic (whether you tap it, or Talk to Atelier or “Start listening when I open Atelier” opens it for you) is sent to OpenAI (or to Google’s Gemini when OpenAI is busy, unavailable or can’t read the recording) only to turn it into text in your message box. Atelier doesn’t store the recording or a separate copy of the transcription on its servers. If you send the text, it becomes part of your conversation and follows the thread-storage choices below. On Android and desktop, your browser’s own speech recognition is used first, under its maker’s terms.
  • Look up — when you select a short phrase in a finished answer, that phrase (up to 120 characters) is sent to Atelier’s server, which asks Wikipedia for a public summary and preview image (see Sharing). Depending on your Look up setting this happens automatically, only when you tap Look up, or never.
  • Profile and memory — optional information you add in the “You” panel (name, a description of yourself, writing samples, a style guide, and remembered facts), plus facts Atelier notes from your conversations (never from text that arrived through a link or a share), which you can view and delete.
  • Imported history — if you choose to import a ChatGPT/Claude data export or Claude Code session files, Atelier reads the messages you wrote in them to build your profile. The files are opened in your browser and are never uploaded or stored as files. To build the profile, the messages you wrote — newest first, each cut to its first 900 characters, up to about 150,000 characters in all for testers (280,000 for the owner) — are sent to an AI model for analysis (Google’s Gemini when it is available, otherwise another of the models listed under Sharing), together with, for Claude Code sessions, the last folder name of up to 25 projects you worked in and how many prompts each had (for example “Acme-Site (212 prompts)”) — never the drive, the folders above it or your home folder, whose name is usually your computer’s user name. The profile, writing style and remembered facts that come back are saved like the rest of your “You” panel (on the server, so they sync between your devices; for testers, in your tester record). Nothing else from the files is sent or kept by this profile import.
  • Imported Claude chats — if you choose Settings → Your data → Import Claude chats, Atelier opens your claude.ai data export in your browser and saves each conversation (your messages, Claude’s replies and reasoning, the names of attached files and a short excerpt of their extracted text, each cut to a size limit) as a thread on this device. Nothing is sent to an AI model while importing. These imported threads sync to your other devices when thread sync is enabled; for testers, private sync is off until you explicitly enable it. Afterwards they are treated like your other threads: when you continue one, its earlier turns go to the model you are using, and when you ask the accounts agent about your Claude history, the passages it finds go to the agent’s model. Atelier never signs in to claude.ai or reads it directly.
  • Voluntary feedback — if you choose Send feedback, Atelier stores the message and category you submit so the studio owner can read and respond to bugs, confusing behavior and suggestions. You may choose to attach a PNG, JPEG or WebP screenshot under 350 KB and include basic diagnostics: the active mode, app version, online status and screen size. Prompts, conversation contents and private connected-account details are not automatically collected with feedback. Review your message and screenshot before submitting them; they contain whatever you choose to include.

If you sign in as a LinkedIn tester

Tester access is currently closed. No one can sign in as a tester right now, and a tester sign-in cookie still in a browser is no longer accepted and is removed the next time that browser opens Atelier. This section describes what applies while tester access is open; the retention and deletion rules for tester records that already exist (sections 6 and 8) apply either way.

  • LinkedIn sign-in — Atelier asks LinkedIn only for your basic profile and email address (the openid profile email permissions) and keeps your LinkedIn member ID, name, email address and profile photo link. It never asks to post on your behalf. The LinkedIn access token is used once at sign-in and is not stored. If your sign-in is turned away, only a short-lived note of it is kept (see “Refused tester sign-ins” in section 6).
  • Usage ledger — for each request, how much it cost and when, so your daily and monthly allowance can be enforced. The ledger holds amounts and dates, not prompts or answers. Server-stored profile and memory, optional synced threads, voluntary feedback and the Look up cache are described separately below.
  • Your profile, style and memory — what you keep in the “You” panel (name, a description of yourself, writing samples, a style guide, learned facts and remembered items) is stored on the server in your tester record so Atelier can use it in your answers.
  • Your threads — conversations and generated media are saved in a browser database for your LinkedIn account, separately from other accounts on that browser. Private thread sync is off until you explicitly enable it. With your consent, threads and their images and videos are also saved in a separate, private Cloudflare R2 storage namespace for that LinkedIn account so you can continue on another device. Each tester account has a 1 GiB cloud-storage limit. Older threads in the previous shared browser database stay local unless you explicitly select and import them into your account.
  • The studio owner’s threads — the studio owner’s own threads, including their images and videos, also sync privately to the owner’s Cloudflare storage behind the owner’s passcode. Tester sync uses a separate namespace and does not give testers access to the owner’s threads.
  • Prompts — what you send goes to the AI provider of the model chosen for that request, only to produce the answer (see Sharing).
  • Voice dictation — recordings are transcribed as described above and not stored; the usage ledger records only what each one cost.
  • Look up — free: it never touches your usage ledger. It starts on “On tap”, so a phrase you select is sent only when you tap Look up (you can change this in Settings). Its answer and preview image are cached briefly at Cloudflare for you alone (see “Look-up cache” in section 6).
  • Video files — a video you attach for analysis may be uploaded to Google’s Gemini Files API, which holds it for up to 48 hours and then deletes it.
  • Sign-in cookies — two strictly necessary cookies: __Host-atelier_tester keeps you signed in for up to 30 days, and __Host-atelier_li lasts at most 10 minutes and is used only to finish the LinkedIn sign-in round trip. Neither is used for tracking.

Connected accounts (only if you connect them)

Only the owner can connect accounts or install the browser extension; testers can’t.

  • Google (Gmail, Calendar, Drive and Photos, through one Google sign-in) — see Google user data below.
  • Canva (through Canva sign-in) — Atelier lists and searches your designs and reads their details, exports a design you choose to import into Atelier, and — when you tap Send to Canva or approve it on an approval card — uploads an image or creates a design in your Canva account. It asks Canva for these permissions: read design details (design:meta:read), read and create design content (design:content:read, design:content:write), read and upload images (asset:read, asset:write) and read your basic Canva profile (profile:read, for the account’s display name). Only the Canva sign-in tokens and that display name are stored on the server; a design you import is saved in your browser, like the rest of your Library.
  • Slack, GitHub, Stripe, Cloudflare, Railway — when connected, Atelier reads the data needed to answer a request (for example, messages matching a search, open pull requests, recent payments, DNS records or deployments).
  • Browser extension — if you install the optional Atelier Browser extension, Atelier can list your open tabs and read the page you ask about. Clicks and typing happen only after you approve each one, and the extension never enters passwords or payment details.

Technical data

  • Our host, Cloudflare, processes standard request data (such as IP address and request logs) to operate and secure the service. We use IP addresses to block repeated wrong-passcode attempts and to limit repeated sign-in attempts and security reports. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies; the only cookies are the tester sign-in cookies described above.
  • Page analytics — Atelier’s pages load Cloudflare Web Analytics, a small script from static.cloudflareinsights.com that counts visits. It sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device (no local storage) and, per Cloudflare, does not fingerprint you by your IP address, browser details or anything else. For each page view it reports the page address, the site that referred you, your country, your browser, operating system and device type (desktop, mobile or tablet), and how fast the page loaded. The owner sees only aggregate statistics in Cloudflare’s dashboard — visits, page views, top pages, referring sites, countries and browsers — never linked to your sign-in, your prompts or your tester record. The owner can switch it off in Cloudflare at any time.
  • Fonts — Atelier’s pages, this one included, load their typefaces from Google Fonts: the stylesheet from fonts.googleapis.com and the font files from fonts.gstatic.com. Your browser fetches them directly from Google, before you sign in and on each page load, so Google receives your IP address, your browser details (user agent) and the address of the site that asked for them. Google Fonts sets no cookies, and Atelier sends Google nothing else for this. A Build preview you run may likewise load fonts and code libraries from Google Fonts or the jsDelivr and unpkg CDNs.
  • Security reports — Atelier’s pages carry a Content Security Policy. If your browser sees a page try to load something the policy doesn’t list, it may send Atelier a short automatic report. The browser’s report can include the full page address, the page you came from and a snippet of the blocked code; Atelier discards all of that. It records one line per report with only: the policy rule involved, the website (domain only) of the blocked item or a word such as “inline”, the Atelier page and script file without anything after “?” or “#”, a line and column number, and whether the policy was enforced. Atelier does not record your IP address, browser or device details (user agent), the referring page, query strings or any page content with it, and the report is never linked to your sign-in. Your IP address is used only briefly, as a counter key that stops one connection from flooding reports. These lines are kept in Cloudflare’s Worker logs for at most 7 days and are used only to keep Atelier secure and working.

How Google user data is used

  • Only to provide the user-facing features you request: searching, reading, summarizing, drafting and — with your approval — sending email; listing and — with your approval — creating or deleting calendar events; searching and reading Drive files and — with your approval — creating a Google Doc; and attaching the photos you pick.
  • Email, calendar, Drive and photo content needed for a request is sent to the AI model provider selected for that request (see Sharing) solely to generate the response you asked for.
  • It is not used for advertising, not sold, not used to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine-learning models, and not read by humans unless you explicitly ask for help with a specific message, it is necessary for security or legal compliance, or the data is aggregated and anonymized for internal operations.

How Google user data is stored

  • Atelier stores only the OAuth refresh token (and a short-lived access token) in encrypted Cloudflare storage so it can call Google on your behalf. Email, calendar, Drive and photo content is not stored on our servers, except where the studio owner’s own answers quote it inside the owner’s synced threads.
  • Answers that quote or summarize this content, and photos you attach, are saved in your conversation history in your own browser, where you can delete them.
Limited Use disclosure. Atelier’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

4. How we use data

  • To answer your requests and perform the actions you ask for (chat, code, images, video, ideas, apps, and account tasks).
  • To personalize answers using your profile, memory and writing style.
  • To keep the service secure and working (for example, rate-limiting passcode attempts and diagnosing errors).
  • To see, in aggregate, which pages are used and how fast they load (Cloudflare Web Analytics, without cookies).
  • For testers: to enforce each tester’s spending allowance and the shared monthly tester budget.
  • To sync threads across devices when enabled, and to review feedback you voluntarily submit.

We do not use your data for advertising, profiling for third parties, or selling.

6. Where data is stored and for how long

  • In your browser: conversations, generated media and settings — kept until you delete them (Settings → Your data → Clear this device, or delete individual threads). Tester threads use a local database tied to the signed-in LinkedIn account; threads left in the previous shared database remain local unless you explicitly select and import them.
  • The studio owner’s threads: the studio owner’s own threads, including their images and videos, also sync privately to the owner’s Cloudflare storage behind the owner’s passcode, until the owner deletes them. The original of a video attached to a question isn’t synced (only the frames and preview Atelier took from it are). On a phone whose browser reports mobile data or Data Saver (Chrome on Android does; Safari on iPhone doesn’t, so there videos sync on any network), videos over 10 MB wait for Wi-Fi before they upload or download, unless the owner turns on “Download videos on mobile data”. A deleted thread can be restored for 30 days; after that its saved copies are removed, except copies of images, videos and long texts (over about 32 KB, such as long answers or pasted documents), which remain in that private storage until a later clean-up removes the ones no thread uses. Tester sync uses a separate namespace and does not give testers access to the owner’s threads.
  • Tester thread sync: off until you explicitly enable private sync. Synced conversations, imported chats and media are held in private Cloudflare R2 storage for your LinkedIn account, with a 1 GiB limit that counts everything kept there (including the saved copies described below) and up to 500 threads. The same media and mobile-data rules described for owner sync apply. Turning sync off in Settings → Your data stops new transfers but keeps existing cloud copies. Clear this device removes local data only; signing in to the same LinkedIn account brings synced copies back when sync is enabled. A synced thread deleted from the Threads drawer can be restored for 30 days. The copy of a deleted thread kept for restoring, and the earlier versions Atelier keeps of edited or restored threads, are removed 30 days after they were saved (a restored thread’s copy goes when you restore it). Copies of images, videos and long texts (over about 32 KB) aren’t removed when a thread is deleted: they stay in that private storage, counting toward the limit, until the entire tester cloud namespace is removed when the tester record expires after 90 days of inactivity (or sooner on request). If cloud clean-up fails or needs more than one pass, the server retries it before deleting the tester record.
  • Voluntary feedback: the message, category, any screenshot you chose and any basic diagnostics you included are kept in Cloudflare storage for up to 90 days after submission, with an expiry set when each submission is saved. Older submissions may be removed sooner to keep the feedback inbox within its limits. Only the studio owner can read the feedback inbox.
  • Video Remix (this device): the attached video (up to 500 MB), new shots, screenshots you add, plan drafts and finished cuts are kept in this browser’s IndexedDB (keys starting rx:). The copy of the video, drafts and cuts are removed once the remix hasn’t changed for 7 days (checked when Atelier opens); new shots (which were paid for) stay while their thread exists; everything is removed once its thread is deleted (shots still being filmed are collected first so their cost settles), and Settings → Your data → Clear this device removes them all. None of these files is synced on its own: only the remix’s plan, settings, each new shot’s status and small preview stills travel with the owner’s thread, and a finished cut under 16 MB is added to its thread (so it appears in the Library) and then syncs like any other video in a thread; a bigger cut stays on this device.
  • Read aloud clips: audio made for Read aloud is cached only on this device (browser Cache Storage, up to about 30 MB or 200 clips) until it is cleared with Settings → Your data → Clear this device, or when a tester signs out.
  • Dictation recordings: never saved. A recording stays in the page’s memory only until it has been transcribed (or, if sending it fails, until you record again or close Atelier), and Atelier’s server passes it to the transcription provider without storing it.
  • Quick launch (this device only): something you share to Atelier from another app (or that a website sends to it the same way) waits in browser Cache Storage until Atelier opens it (at most one video or 4 photos plus its text). It can be used for 30 minutes; that limit is checked when Atelier reads it, not by a timer, so it is deleted once opened, the next time Atelier opens after that, when you sign out, and by Clear this device. A message you typed or dictated but haven’t sent is kept in this browser’s local storage so it survives the phone reopening Atelier: it can come back for 6 hours, and is deleted when you send it, the next time Atelier opens, when you sign out, and by Clear this device. A launch that arrives while you are signed out (a shortcut, a link’s text or a share) can continue for 15 minutes after you sign in; it is deleted when you next sign in or open Atelier signed in, and by Clear this device. Your Quick launch choices, and the private key of a “Send without a tap” link, stay in this browser until you sign out or clear this device (the key is also in the link you paste into your Shortcut). Each Shortcut run opens that link with your words in it, so your browser’s History (and History sync, such as iCloud or Chrome sync) keeps the key and those words; use Settings → Quick launch → New link if anyone else can see that history. Atelier Assist, the Android side-button app, works the same way: each request opens Atelier at a link with that key and your words in it, so Chrome’s History (and Chrome sync) keeps them too; New link under Quick launch → Atelier Assist retires that key. Atelier Assist turns your voice into text with your phone’s speech service, which may use Google’s or your phone maker’s servers, before the words reach Atelier; the app itself keeps only the key. None of this reaches Atelier’s server unless you send it as a message, and the key never does. One exception: a share that arrives while Atelier’s offline helper (its service worker) isn’t running — for example just after you clear this site’s data, or when a very large video stops it — is sent by your browser to Atelier’s server instead, which discards it without reading or storing it and asks you to share again.
  • Look-up cache (Cloudflare): the Wikipedia answer to a look-up (which repeats the phrase you selected, up to 120 characters) is kept for up to 24 hours (1 hour when Wikipedia has no article for it), and its preview image for up to 7 days, in the Cloudflare edge cache of the data centre that served you. This applies to testers and the owner alike. It is stored separately from your profile and memory, optional synced threads and voluntary feedback. Each entry is filed under a one-way (SHA-256) hash of who asked together with the phrase and the Wikipedia language (for an image, who asked and the image’s file name), so it is only ever served back to the same person — no other Atelier user can see or detect what you looked up. Nothing else from the answer you selected it in is kept.
  • Cloudflare (server): your profile/memory (so it syncs between your devices) and connection tokens — kept until you delete or disconnect them.
  • Tester records: your LinkedIn ID, name, email and photo link, your usage ledger and your profile, style and memory expire after 90 days without using Atelier, or sooner on request. Any tester cloud-sync namespace is purged before its expired tester record is deleted; clean-up is retried if it fails or needs more than one pass.
  • Refused tester sign-ins: if a LinkedIn sign-in is turned away (all tester spots are taken, tester access is paused, or your access was revoked), Atelier keeps a note of the most recent such attempt — the LinkedIn member ID, name and profile photo link LinkedIn returned, and the time — so the owner can see it in the Testers panel and decide whether to open a spot. Your email address is not part of it. Only the latest attempt is kept (a newer one replaces it), and it is deleted 7 days after the attempt, or sooner on request.
  • Gemini Files API: videos uploaded for analysis are deleted by Google within 48 hours.
  • Runway (owner only): Runway’s links to finished videos expire within 48 hours; Atelier keeps its own copy on the owner’s device, not on its server.
  • xAI (owner only): xAI’s links to finished Grok videos are temporary; Atelier keeps its own copy of a Grok image or video on the owner’s device, not on its server.
  • AI providers: may retain request data briefly under their own API policies for abuse monitoring; they do not use it for advertising.
  • Operational logs: short-lived request logs kept by Cloudflare for reliability and security. Atelier’s own log lines, including security reports, are kept for at most 7 days.
  • Page analytics: Cloudflare Web Analytics data is kept by Cloudflare under its own retention and shown to the owner only as aggregate page-view and page-speed statistics in Cloudflare’s dashboard; nothing is stored on your device for it.

7. Security

Traffic is encrypted with HTTPS, and browsers are told to use only HTTPS for Atelier (HSTS). API keys and account tokens are stored as encrypted server-side secrets and never sent to your browser. Access requires the owner’s passcode, with automatic lockout after repeated wrong attempts, or a LinkedIn tester sign-in while tester access is open (it is currently closed); tester sessions are stored only as a one-way hash, and testers can never reach the owner’s accounts, tools or profile. Actions that send, post, pay or change something require your explicit approval.

8. Your choices and deletion

  • Delete conversations and media: delete threads in the app, or use Settings → Your data → Clear this device. With thread sync on, Clear this device leaves synced copies in private cloud storage: delete synced threads from the Threads drawer instead (they can be restored for 30 days from Settings → Your data → Recently deleted).
  • Turn tester sync off: Settings → Your data → Sync across your devices. This stops new transfers without deleting your cloud copies. Sign in to the same LinkedIn account and enable sync to resume. Threads that have never synced stay on the device where they were made unless you export or import them.
  • Delete profile and memory: edit or remove entries in the “You” panel.
  • Disconnect Google: Settings → Connections → Disconnect (this deletes the stored token), and/or revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Disconnect Canva: Settings → Connections → Disconnect (this revokes Atelier’s access at Canva and deletes the stored tokens).
  • Disconnect other services: revoke the token in that service’s settings; Atelier then loses access immediately.
  • Testers: sign out in Settings → Access. Your tester record, usage ledger, profile and any private cloud-sync namespace are removed after 90 days of inactivity, with cloud clean-up retried before the expired record is deleted. To request earlier account and cloud-data deletion, email cole@ciprari.ai. You can also remove Atelier from your LinkedIn account’s permitted services.
  • Request deletion of everything: email cole@ciprari.ai and we will delete server-side data within 30 days.
